# Action, VFX and physics craft (distilled from the Seedance 2.0 master guide)

## Specificity is everything

Generic verbs generate mush. Every action needs: who (exact subject), what
(concrete physical verb), how (manner/force), where in frame, and what it
causes. "She swings" → "She pivots on her back foot and swings the pipe in a
flat arc into his raised forearm; the impact knocks him a half-step sideways."

## Physics beats

Every powerful moment is a chain: cause → energy transfer → visible
consequence → settle. Weight, momentum and follow-through sell realism:
cloth and hair lag behind motion, mass resists sudden stops, impacts shed
debris/dust/sparks, water and glass react in the same shot. Describe the
consequence explicitly — models render what is written, not what is implied.

## VFX vocabulary that reads well

- Energy/magic: describe as physical light with color, source and behavior —
  "cyan light fractures along hairline cracks in the air, casting hard rim
  light on his face" beats "magical energy appears".
- Reality breaks / portals: give the effect a material metaphor (shattering
  glass, tearing paper, folding cloth, static interference) and a direction
  of propagation; state what is visible through the break.
- Particles: name size, density, motion (embers drift up; shards hang then
  reverse; dust blooms outward at knee height).
- Impact frames: one beat may hold a near-still high-contrast frame at the
  hit, then resume motion — cinematic and cheap.
- Transformations: anchor start and end states and one continuous visible
  path between them; morphs without a path look like cross-fades.

## Fight tempo — fights play at fight speed

The most common generated-fight failure is SLOW SPARRING: technically correct
moves at rehearsal speed, watched by a polite camera. Cures, all of which are
writable:

- **Verbs carry the speed.** drives / slams / whips / hurls / snaps — never
  "moves toward", "begins to", "prepares to". If a verb could describe tai
  chi, replace it.
- **Every strike buys a consequence** in the same shot: a body staggers three
  steps, a rack goes over, dust jumps off the floor, breath knocks out
  visibly. A hit with no consequence reads as a miss.
- **Speed contrast sells force.** One slow beat (circling, a held guard, a
  breath) makes the next exchange read twice as fast. All-fast is as flat as
  all-slow.
- **The camera is a participant, not a witness**: tracking shots with large
  amplitude at fast speed through exchanges, a fast push-in on a landed hit,
  a small fast shake at the moment of impact, a whip pan to the arriving
  attacker. The static camera appears exactly twice in a good fight: the
  geography-establishing wide, and the aftermath.
- **Exchanges cut short** — 2-3.5 seconds a shot. A fight shot past 4 seconds
  is either geography or aftermath.
- **Impact frames**: one near-still high-contrast frame at the biggest hit,
  then resume motion.

## Team coordination — abilities as one move

When two or more characters fight (or work) as a unit, write COMBO shots: one
shot in which both actions are named as a single coordinated move with a
causal link — "Kess freezes the sentry's optics in a burst of static; through
the blind spot Mara is already sliding under its arm". The pass-off is the
content of the shot: A creates the opening, B spends it, in that order, in
frame together at least once per sequence. Teams that never share a frame
read as strangers taking turns.

Map energy to sections explicitly: verses carry story and quiet tension
(longer shots, slower moves, distance between characters); pre-chorus
tightens (push-ins, shorter beats); chorus is the widest, boldest, most
kinetic imagery; a bridge inverts (slow motion, silence-like stillness,
palette flip); the final chorus must top every previous image. Never spend
the biggest visual early.

## Multi-world / portal-chase structure

Give each world ONE dominant color + material + physics rule, state it in
the first beat that enters it, and keep characters' identity anchors
unchanged through every world. Transitions between worlds are their own
beats (the effect is the action). Escalate world strangeness toward the
climax; return to the origin world (or a transformed version of it) for the
resolution so the journey reads as an arc, not a slideshow.
