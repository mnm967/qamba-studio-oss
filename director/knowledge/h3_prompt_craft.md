# MiniMax H3 — beat-writing craft

How to write scenes and beats that compile into strong H3 passes. (The final
prompt format is produced by a deterministic compiler — never write it by
hand; these notes shape the *content* of beats.)

## Camera language

Describe camera as type + amplitude + speed in plain prose, e.g. "slow push-in
from medium shot to close-up", "fast lateral tracking at street level",
"overhead crane pulling up, moderate speed", "static locked-off wide". H3
responds to named shot sizes (extreme wide, wide, medium, medium close-up,
close-up, extreme close-up), angles (low angle, high angle, overhead, dutch),
and moves (push-in, pull-back, lateral track, orbit, crane, handheld drift,
whip pan). Avoid lens jargon (no "24mm", no "dolly zoom") — plain prose wins.
One camera idea per beat; compound moves read as mush.

## Identity discipline

A character's look must be re-stated with the same concrete attributes each
time they appear: hair length+color+style, eye color, one distinguishing mark,
the exact named outfit pieces with colors, accessories. Six to eight
attributes is the sweet spot — fewer drifts, more dilutes. Never paraphrase
an identity line; repeat it verbatim and let the action vary around it.

## Action writing

Present tense, concrete, visual, causal: what moves, what it hits, what
reacts. "Rei vaults the railing, boots skidding on wet concrete, and catches
the antenna mast one-handed" — not "Rei escapes dramatically". Name physical
contact points and surfaces; H3 grounds motion on them. Keep one primary
action per beat plus at most one secondary detail. Faces: name the emotion
AND its physical tell ("jaw tightens", "eyes widen").

## Sound-aware beats

H3 generates synchronized audio. Every beat should imply its diegetic sound:
surfaces (rain on metal), props (glass shattering), space (echoing stairwell).
Spoken lines carry a delivery ("whispered, shaking", "flat, cold"). When
nobody speaks and no lyric plays, say what fills the silence. For locked
music-video audio, mouths stay closed on instrumental stretches — write
action that reads well without singing.

## Pacing and duration

Most beats land at 3–5 seconds. Under 2s only for accents (impact, insert,
whip cut). 8–12s for one-take choreography or slow emotional holds. A 15s
block fits 3–4 beats comfortably; give each beat one clear visual change so
cuts feel earned. Alternate shot sizes between consecutive beats — wide →
close → medium reads as rhythm; three mediums in a row reads as dead air.

## Continuity endings

End every scene's final beat on a *describable* composition: where the
subject is in frame, facing which way, doing what — because the next block
literally opens on that frame. "She faces the skyline from behind, right hand
raised toward the tower" chains cleanly; "the fight continues" chains as
chaos. Mid-gesture endings are fine only when the next beat continues the
same gesture. Fresh locations cut hard: end the prior scene closed (a look,
a door, a held object), not reaching.
