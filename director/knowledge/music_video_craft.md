# Music video craft

## Structure maps the song

Break the track into sections (intro, verse 1, pre-chorus, chorus, verse 2,
bridge, final chorus, outro) and give each section a visual identity: a
location, a palette, a camera behavior. Verses develop narrative or
performance intimacy; choruses go biggest — widest shots, boldest color,
fastest cuts or the one-take showpiece. Repeat imagery across choruses with
escalation (same rooftop, more lights, more rain, closer). The bridge is the
rule-breaker: invert the palette, flip to slow motion, strip to a single
close-up.

## Performance and narrative interleave

Two strands: the artist performing (singing to camera or into the world) and
the story imagery the song means. Alternate them so neither goes stale; let
them converge by the final chorus (the performer inside the story space).
Performance shots must lip-sync the exact lyric of that moment — the beat's
sung line drives mouth movement — so place lyric lines precisely on their
timestamps. During instrumental stretches mouths stay closed; give the body
the rhythm instead: walking on the beat, lights pulsing, cuts landing on
drum hits.

## Cutting to the beat

Cuts land on downbeats; movement inside the shot carries the eighth notes.
Fast sections tolerate 1–2s shots; slow sections want long holds with
internal motion (steadicam drift, rain, passing lights). Beat-snapped
boundaries matter more than shot variety rules — a cut slightly early reads
as sloppy, slightly late as heavy.

## Motif and payoff

Pick one strong visual motif from the song's central image (a color, an
object, a gesture) and return to it at least three times: introduce in the
intro, complicate in the bridge, pay off in the final chorus. The last shot
of the video should rhyme with the first — same framing, changed meaning.
